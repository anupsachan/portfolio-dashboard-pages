Breakout

BuySwing days → weeks
In plain English

Imagine a stock that ran up like a rocket, then took a breather — it drifted sideways in a tight range, like a sprinter catching their breath at the top of a hill. When it finally pushes above that resting zone on strong volume, you're betting the run isn't over. You're buying the second leg of the staircase, not the first.

Big run up
30–100%+ in weeks
→
Tight rest
2 weeks – 2 months
→
Break above range
Enter on strength

How to find candidates

  • Scan for stocks in the top 1–2% of performers over 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months — these are today's leaders.
  • Run scans weekly.
  • Look for: (1) a prior big move, (2) orderly pullback with higher lows and tightening range, (3) price "surfing" the rising 10- and 20-day moving averages.

How to trade it

  • Entry: Buy when price breaks above the opening range high (ORH) — first 1-min, 5-min, or 60-min candle high after the open. Or enter when the daily chart shows a clean breakout.
  • Stop: Low of the day. Keep stop ≤ the stock's average daily range (ADR/ATR) so risk/reward stays sane.
  • Take profit: Sell ⅓ to ½ after 3–5 days, move stop to breakeven. Trail the rest with the 10-day MA (beginners) or 20-day if the stock moves slower — exit on first close below the MA.

Pre-market checklist

  • Stock in top 1–2% on 1/3/6-month scans
  • Prior 30–100%+ move, then 2+ weeks consolidation
  • Higher lows, tightening range, riding 10/20 MA
  • Watchlist + alerts set before open
  • Share size calculated (risk ≤ 1% of account)
  • Stop width ≤ ADR/ATR of the stock

Episodic Pivot (EP)

BuySwing weeks → months
In plain English

A stock nobody cared about (or forgot about) suddenly reports blowout earnings or news. The market wakes up and gaps the stock up 10%+ on huge volume — like a quiet restaurant getting a Michelin star overnight. You're buying the moment the market re-prices the company, not chasing a stock that's already been hot for months.

What counts as a real EP

  • Gap up 10%+ on the news.
  • Massive volume — ideally average daily volume traded in the first 15–30 minutes, or heavy pre-market volume.
  • For earnings EPs: big growth (mid/high or triple-digit EPS & revenue) and a significant beat vs analyst estimates.
  • Best when the stock has not already rallied hard over the prior 3–6 months — the surprise should be genuine.
  • A gap with big numbers but no volume or immediate sell-off is NOT an EP (see NVDA 2016 middle arrow in the blog).

How to trade it

  • Spot it: After-hours or pre-market — look for gappers on earnings/news.
  • Entry: ORH (same as breakouts). Stop at low of the day.
  • Exit: Trail with 10- or 20-day MA once price moves above your initial stop level. Create your own sell rules — these can run for months.

Pre-market checklist

  • Gap ≥ 10% on meaningful catalyst
  • Volume surging (pre-market or first 15–30 min)
  • Earnings: strong growth + beat (if applicable)
  • Stock wasn't already extended 3–6 months
  • Not buying a gap that immediately fades
  • ORH entry plan + stop at LOD defined

Parabolic Short (or Long)

Short (or bounce long)Days
In plain English

A stock goes straight up so fast it looks vertical — like pulling a rubber band too far. Eventually it snaps back. You wait for the first sign of weakness (not the top — let impatient shorts get burned first), then bet on a pullback toward the 10- or 20-day moving average. The long version is the mirror: after a parabolic crash of 50–60%+, buy the violent bounce.

Short setup criteria

  • Stock up 50–100%+ in days/weeks (large caps) or 300–1000%+ (small caps).
  • Often 3–5+ green days in a row, or accelerating after a long trend.

How to trade the short

  • Don't be early. Wait for opening range lows, first red 5-min candle, or a failed bounce at VWAP.
  • Entry: Short on ORL or VWAP fail (first red candle into VWAP that can't reclaim it).
  • Stop: High of the day, or VWAP reclaim — keep stops tight and defined.
  • Target: 10- and 20-day moving averages (where bounces usually happen).
  • Expect ~5–10× risk/reward (not 30–50× like breakouts/EPs), but higher win rate if patient.

Parabolic long (rare)

  • After a successful parabolic short drops 50–60%+ in a few days, look for ORH or first green 5-min candle.
  • These can bounce 50–100% in days — but they're less common.

Pre-market checklist

  • Stock extended 50%+ (or 300%+ small cap) in short window
  • Multiple consecutive up days / parabolic curve visible
  • Waiting for weakness — NOT shorting into strength
  • Entry trigger defined (ORL, red 5-min, VWAP fail)
  • Stop = HOD or VWAP reclaim (tight!)
  • Target = 10/20-day MA zone

Universal risk & sizing rules

In plain English

You don't need to win every trade. You need small losses and occasional huge winners. Risk rules are the seatbelt — they keep one bad trade from wrecking the account. Even if you're wrong 70% of the time, a few 10–20× winners can still make you very profitable.

Position sizing math (simple)

  • Decide how much you're willing to lose if stopped out — e.g. 1% of $100k = $1,000.
  • Entry $50, stop $48 → $2 risk per share → buy 500 shares max ($1,000 ÷ $2).
  • Cap position at 30% of account even if math allows more.

Key concepts glossary

  • ORH / ORL — Opening Range High/Low: the high or low of the first 1-, 5-, or 60-minute candle after the market opens. Used as entry triggers.
  • ADR / ATR — Average Daily Range / Average True Range: how much the stock typically moves per day. Your stop shouldn't be wider than this.
  • 10/20-day MA — Moving averages on the daily chart. Stocks in strong trends "surf" above them; a close below = trend weakening.
  • VWAP — Volume-weighted average price for the day. Parabolic shorts often fail at VWAP bounces.
  • DTE — Days to expiration (for options). Not part of Qullamaggie's stock setups but relevant to your book.
